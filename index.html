JW

I build structural macroeconomic models to answer three questions: how monetary and fiscal policy are transmitted in an open economy; how external shocks move saving, the external balances and the room for manoeuvre left to the authorities; and what demographic change does to the macroeconomy. The empirical work draws on advanced economies and on resource-dependent African economies.

Je construis des modèles macroéconomiques structurels pour répondre à trois questions : comment se transmettent les politiques monétaire et budgétaire en économie ouverte ; comment les chocs externes déplacent l’épargne, les soldes extérieurs et la marge de manœuvre des autorités ; et ce que le changement démographique fait à la macroéconomie. Mes applications empiriques portent sur les économies avancées et sur les économies africaines tributaires des ressources naturelles.

I am an associate professor in the Department of Economics at the University of Kinshasa and a visiting professor at the Université Officielle de Bukavu and the Université Évangélique en Afrique, where I teach computable general equilibrium modelling. In Quebec, I am a lecturer in the Department of Economics and Administration at the Université du Québec à Chicoutimi, and a research professional in macroeconomics at CIRANO and at the Chair in Macroeconomics and Forecasting of UQAM.

Je suis professeur associé au Département d’économie de l’Université de Kinshasa et professeur visiteur à l’Université Officielle de Bukavu ainsi qu’à l’Université Évangélique en Afrique, où j’enseigne la modélisation en équilibre général calculable. Au Québec, je suis chargé de cours au Département d’économie et d’administration de l’Université du Québec à Chicoutimi et professionnel de recherche en macroéconomie au CIRANO et à la Chaire de recherche en macroéconomie de l’UQAM.

I work on both sides of macroeconomics. On the theoretical side, I build DSGE and overlapping-generations models to trace how households, policy and external constraints interact over the life cycle. On the empirical side, I take those mechanisms to the data with panel and time-varying-parameter methods. Each side informs the other, since a model indicates which correlations should appear in the data, and the estimates then discipline the next model.

Je travaille des deux côtés de la macroéconomie. Du côté théorique, je construis des modèles DSGE et à générations imbriquées pour suivre l’interaction entre les ménages, la politique économique et les contraintes extérieures sur le cycle de vie. Du côté empirique, je confronte ces mécanismes aux données par des méthodes de panel et à paramètres variables dans le temps. Chaque versant éclaire l’autre, puisque le modèle indique quelles corrélations devraient apparaître dans les données et que les estimations disciplinent ensuite le modèle suivant.

Four strands organise the work. The first is monetary and fiscal policy. I ask how the policy rate and the public budget transmit to output, prices and the external accounts, and what limits that transmission once an economy is open to trade and to global financial conditions. This question runs through both the models I build and the estimates I take to the data.

Quatre axes structurent ce travail. Le premier porte sur les politiques monétaire et budgétaire. J’examine comment le taux directeur et le budget public se transmettent à la production, aux prix et aux comptes extérieurs, et ce qui limite cette transmission dès lors qu’une économie s’ouvre aux échanges et aux conditions financières mondiales. Cette question traverse aussi bien les modèles que je construis que les estimations que je confronte aux données.

The second looks at small open economies under partial dollarization, using the Democratic Republic of Congo as a working laboratory for how external monetary shocks travel through exchange rates, commodity prices and reserves.

Le deuxième porte sur les petites économies ouvertes partiellement dollarisées, avec la République démocratique du Congo comme laboratoire pour comprendre la transmission des chocs monétaires externes par les taux de change, les prix des matières premières et les réserves.

The third concerns financial factors in macroeconomic modelling and the welfare costs of inflation, the subject of my work on markups and trend inflation.

Le troisième porte sur les facteurs financiers dans la modélisation macroéconomique et sur les coûts en bien-être de l’inflation, objet de mes travaux sur les markups et la tendance inflationniste.

The fourth is the longest-running. With the Groupe MEGC at the University of Kinshasa, I apply computable general equilibrium analysis to the Congolese economy. That work built a social accounting matrix for the country and used it to evaluate regional integration, trade liberalisation, agricultural investment and poverty reduction. It is also what I now teach in Bukavu.

Le quatrième est le plus ancien. Avec le Groupe MEGC de l’Université de Kinshasa, j’applique l’analyse en équilibre général calculable à l’économie congolaise. Ces travaux ont produit une matrice de comptabilité sociale pour le pays et l’ont utilisée pour évaluer l’intégration régionale, la libéralisation commerciale, l’investissement agricole et la réduction de la pauvreté. C’est aussi ce que j’enseigne aujourd’hui à Bukavu.

Artificial Intelligence, Aging, and the Macroeconomy: A Life-Cycle General Equilibrium Model with Endogenous Fertility, AI Capital, and Longevity

Single-authoredAuteur unique

This paper develops a general equilibrium overlapping-generations model with endogenous fertility, in which firms accumulate both physical and artificial intelligence capital, and uses it to study the macroeconomic transmission of two structural disturbances, an AI technology shock and a longevity shock. The AI shock acts as a capital demand disturbance. It raises all rates of return, most sharply the return to AI capital, reallocates investment from physical to AI capital, and produces a frontloaded output expansion that decays monotonically. The longevity shock acts as a saving supply disturbance. It deepens the aggregate capital stock, compresses returns and the real interest rate, and generates hump-shaped, persistent dynamics. The two shocks move fertility in opposite directions, since AI raises it modestly through an income effect while longevity lowers it by strengthening the life-cycle saving motive and the cost of child-rearing. A forecast-error variance decomposition attributes most aggregate volatility to the longevity shock, while the AI shock dominates the variance of the return to AI capital. Fertility is strongly countercyclical and almost perfectly negatively correlated with hours worked, placing household time allocation at the centre of the mechanism. A robustness analysis confirms that these conclusions reflect structural properties of the model. Variation in the capital share and in the persistence of the AI shock leaves the signs of the wage, fertility, output and consumption responses unchanged, and only the elasticity of substitution between labour and AI can reverse them, beyond a threshold well above standard empirical estimates.

Transmission of U.S. Monetary Policy Shocks to a Dollarized, Resource-Dependent Economy: Evidence from the Democratic Republic of Congo

Single-authoredAuteur unique

This paper explores the dynamic effects of U.S. monetary tightening on macroeconomic and financial conditions in the Democratic Republic of Congo, a small, resource-dependent and partially dollarized economy. Using a time-varying parameter vector autoregression (TVP-VAR), the study traces the responses of the Congolese franc, copper prices, inflation, broad money and international reserves to an unexpected increase in the U.S. policy rate in January 2019, which coincided with a new political regime. The initial shock induces currency depreciation, a decline in copper prices, short-term disinflation, a contraction in M2 and a fall in reserves. Later dynamics include delayed appreciation, recovery in copper prices and adjustment in monetary aggregates, pointing to compensatory capital flows, export revenues and central bank intervention. The results show how exposed the country is to external financial shocks, how much weight domestic monetary and exchange rate policy carries, and how political regime shifts affect the propagation of those shocks.

International Monetary Policy and Exchange Rate Dynamics in Dollarized Economies: Evidence from the DR Congo

withavec Jean Blaise Nlemfu Mukoko

This paper examines the transmission of external monetary disturbances in a highly dollarized small open economy, using the Democratic Republic of Congo as a representative case. We develop a tractable open-economy model with partial dollarization, incomplete price adjustment and an endogenous risk premium linked to interest rate differentials. Simulations indicate that a U.S. interest rate tightening generates an immediate and persistent depreciation of the domestic currency, alongside rising risk premia and inflationary pressure. Money supply shocks amplify exchange rate and price responses under dollarization as real money demand weakens, while risk premium shocks add volatility through expectation-driven dynamics. Across shock types, domestic monetary policy has limited capacity to offset external disturbances. Dollarization changes how shocks spread, since it reduces policy independence and magnifies the weight of global financial conditions, so stabilization requires tools beyond the interest rate.

International Migration, Ageing, and External Imbalances: A Dynamic Analysis with a Two-Country Life-Cycle Economy

withavec Kevin Moran

This paper develops a two-country, open-economy quantitative model to analyse the macroeconomic implications of demographic shifts such as population ageing, declining population growth, increased longevity and international migration. The model builds on the overlapping-generations framework of Gertler (1999), with young and old households in each country, and calibrates entry, retirement and exit rates to observed population growth, retirement ages and life expectancy. It accommodates differential population growth across countries, migration from developing to developed economies, divergent productivity and imperfect global financial integration. Simulations focus on how ageing, migration and productivity differences affect net exports and the current account in two regions representing the global North and the global South. Demographic trends emerge as a main driver of aggregate savings and external balances. Demographic shocks produce trade and current account surpluses in the North, while productivity differences shape investment patterns across regions.

Demographic Trends, International Migration, and External Imbalances: A Regional Analysis

withavec Kevin Moran

This paper presents an empirical analysis of how global demographic change and international migration affect external balances, grouping countries into advanced, emerging and developing economies and proceeding region by region. Panel models estimate the impact of one demographic factor at a time, whether ageing, fertility, life expectancy or immigration, on one macroeconomic aggregate at a time, such as consumption, savings, investment, the trade balance or the current account. Larger shares of older adults weigh on the trade balance in advanced and emerging economies. Ageing is associated with lower savings rates and current account ratios in emerging and developing economies, while in advanced economies savings tend to rise with life expectancy, ageing and net migration. Net immigration raises savings rates in advanced economies and investment in emerging and developing ones. The results underline why regional conditioning matters, since all regions face demographic change but not at the same scale.

The Effects of Demographic Changes and International Migration on Economic Growth

withavec Kevin Moran

This paper analyses empirically how global demographic change affects GDP growth, region by region across advanced, emerging and developing economies. A decomposition of per capita GDP growth shows that hourly productivity and total hours worked drive growth in advanced economies, while hourly productivity and an expanding working-age population matter most in emerging economies. Falling hours per job have weighed on per capita growth in advanced economies. Panel estimates then take one demographic factor at a time. Larger shares of older adults raise per capita growth in emerging and developing economies but reduce it in advanced ones. Shifts in the working-age population raise per capita GDP across all groups, while a larger youth share lowers it. Net immigration raises per capita growth in advanced and developing economies and lowers it in emerging ones. Taken together, the estimates show that the macroeconomic consequences of demographic shifts depend on the stage of development.

Fertility Decisions, International Migration, Remittances, and Global Imbalances in a Two-Country Life-Cycle Economy

Single-authoredAuteur unique

Using a two-country life-cycle framework with endogenous fertility and remittances, this paper investigates how positive longevity, immigration and productivity shocks affect external imbalances in the North and the South. Positive demographic and productivity shocks improve the North’s trade balance, with demographic shocks the primary driver, followed by productivity, immigration and longevity. Rising productivity and immigration in the North lower the South’s trade balance, while a longevity shock in the North raises it. Longevity, immigration and productivity shocks drive the negative dynamics of the North’s current account, whereas demographic shocks improve the South’s net foreign asset position and current account. Higher longevity in the North raises the old-age dependency ratio, net migration and the fertility of foreign-born workers. Higher immigration raises foreign-born fertility and population growth while lowering native-born fertility. A positive productivity shock lowers fertility, population growth and the old-age dependency ratio.

DR CongoR.D. Congo

The Congolese economy in figuresL’économie congolaise en chiffres

Part of my work takes the Congolese economy as its object, so this page keeps a short public record of the aggregates that discipline it. The country is the case that motivates much of the resource-dependence literature: mineral exports carry the external accounts, the extractive sector carries growth, and the room monetary and fiscal policy has to act moves with the copper and cobalt cycle. The figures below are the published ones, dated and attributed, not my own estimates.

Une partie de mes travaux prend l’économie congolaise pour objet ; cette page en tient donc un relevé public et bref. Le pays est le cas qui motive une bonne part de la littérature sur la dépendance aux ressources : les exportations minières portent les comptes extérieurs, le secteur extractif porte la croissance, et la marge de manœuvre des politiques monétaire et budgétaire suit le cycle du cuivre et du cobalt. Les chiffres ci-dessous sont ceux qui ont été publiés, datés et attribués ; ce ne sont pas mes estimations.
